Mount Hymettus: Why We Love It

From Cholargos and Papagou to Argyroupoli, an introduction to Mount Hymettus and the reasons Athenians love it.

By Δασολόγιο25 August 20253 min read
Topics
ΥμηττόςΑττικήδάσος
ShareFacebookLinkedInX

Mount Hymettus is much more than a mountain beside Athens. It is a living presence connected with the identity and daily life of the city: a green lung, a refuge for wildlife and a landscape carrying memories from ancient marble quarries to Byzantine monasteries.

We also love it for its immediacy. It offers a quick escape from noise, a place for clean air, quiet and a view that lets the city be seen from a different distance. Ecology, history and the personal need for relief meet on its slopes. Protecting Hymettus is therefore both an environmental duty and an act of respect for the city.

A Botanical Treasure

The mountain supports roughly 600 to 700 plant species and subspecies. Around forty are endemic to Greece, while more than forty-four wild orchid species have been recorded, an exceptional concentration for a mountain of this size.

This biodiversity exists beside one of Europe’s largest urban areas. Proximity makes Hymettus especially valuable to residents, but also exposes it to intense pressure. Its management must balance the needs of a fragile ecosystem with recreation for millions of people.

Threats and Wounds

Hymettus was heavily stripped for firewood during the occupation and has repeatedly suffered destructive wildfires. Urban expansion, illegal construction and infrastructure continue to press against its protected zones. Negligence, fragmented management and the effects of a warming climate increase the danger.

Hymettus at a Crossroads

The mountain is at once an archaeological landscape, a Byzantine landmark, a biodiversity refuge and a public place of recreation. Its future depends on coordinated local management, scientific monitoring, firm legal protection and a sustainable balance between conservation, cultural heritage and public access.
