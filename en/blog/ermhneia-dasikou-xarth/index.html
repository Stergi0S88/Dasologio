Understanding Forest Map Codes and Errors

A practical guide to Forest Map codes, common errors and the objection process.

Reading a Forest Map begins with its two-letter codes. Each letter describes the character of the land at a different point in time, normally comparing historical aerial photography with its more recent condition.

What Do the Codes Mean?

Codes such as DD, DA, AD and AA indicate combinations of forest and non-forest character. DD generally describes land that appears forested in both periods. DA indicates land that was historically forested but later shows another use, while AD describes the reverse transition. Additional codes may refer to grassland, reforestation decisions or areas covered by administrative acts.

The code is a starting point, not a complete diagnosis. Boundaries, source imagery and the legal history of the property must also be examined.

Why an Objection Can Be Critical

If an incorrect classification becomes final, it can affect transfers, permits and lawful land use. An objection gives an owner or interested party the opportunity to present historical, technical and legal evidence to the competent committee.

Obvious Errors

Some mistakes may be handled without a substantive objection: a misplaced line, an omitted administrative decision or another error that is objectively demonstrated by the official record. Where interpretation of vegetation or land history is required, the objection route is normally safer.

Procedure and Deadlines

Deadlines and platform availability depend on the area and current administrative stage. Applicants should use the official Forest Map service, verify coordinates, retain proof of submission and provide a complete technical file. Because procedures change, current instructions from the responsible authority should be confirmed before acting.
