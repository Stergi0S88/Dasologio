Forest Property and the Role of the Forester

How land classification affects the use and transfer of property, and how a professional forester can help.

By Δασολόγιο25 August 20253 min read

In Greece, the forest classification of a property can determine whether it may be transferred, developed or used as intended. A plot that appears ordinary on the ground may carry a forest designation on the official map, creating legal and practical restrictions for its owner.

What Is a Forest Map?

A Forest Map records the character of land over time by comparing historical aerial photographs with current evidence. Its purpose is to identify forest and grassland areas and provide a consistent basis for their legal protection.

The map does not decide ownership. It records the character of the land. Nevertheless, its classification has direct consequences for contracts, building permissions, agricultural use and registration in the Cadastre.

Errors and Their Consequences

Common problems include inaccurate boundaries, outdated imagery, agricultural land appearing as forest, or administrative acts that were never incorporated into the map. An incorrect designation can delay a sale, prevent a permit or create uncertainty around an investment.

A forester examines the official codes, historical photographs, topography, vegetation and available administrative documents. Depending on the evidence and procedural stage, the appropriate response may be a request to correct an obvious error, an objection, or a technical report supporting the property’s actual history.

Early professional review is valuable. Before purchasing, selling or planning work on land near a forest area, checking the map and assembling the evidence can prevent expensive delays and protect both the owner and the natural environment.
