Rethinking Our Garden

Why creating a garden with native and local plants is a meaningful act of environmental responsibility.

By Δασολόγιο25 August 20253 min read

A garden is never separate from its surrounding ecosystem. Every choice of plant, soil treatment, watering method and pesticide affects water use, insects, birds and the wider landscape. Rethinking the garden begins by working with local conditions rather than constantly trying to replace them.

Endemic, Native and Local Species

An endemic species occurs naturally only in a limited geographical area. A native species belongs naturally to a wider region without human introduction. A local plant is one adapted to the specific climate, soil and ecological relationships of a place.

These terms are not interchangeable, but all help us choose plants with a genuine connection to the landscape. Selection should also consider the exact site: exposure, elevation, drainage, available space and future size.

Benefits of a Garden with Local Plants

Well-chosen Mediterranean plants generally need less irrigation after establishment and are better prepared for seasonal heat and drought. They provide food and shelter for local pollinators and birds, reduce dependence on fertilisers and help the garden remain resilient.

Diversity matters. Layers of trees, shrubs, herbs and ground cover create more useful habitat than a uniform lawn or a collection of isolated ornamental plants.

The Garden as Personal Action

No private garden will solve the ecological crisis alone, but it can become part of a connected network of small habitats. Avoid invasive species, compost organic material, protect soil, water deeply but less frequently, and accept seasonal change.

A responsible garden is not a frozen display. It is a living place that uses beauty, shade, food and biodiversity to reconnect everyday life with the ecology of its region.
