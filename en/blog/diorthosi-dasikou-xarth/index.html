Forest Map Objection or Correction Request?

The difference between an objection and an obvious-error correction, and what property owners need to know.

Does Your Property Appear as Forest Land?

An unexpected forest designation does not always mean that the map is final or correct. Greek law provides different procedures according to the nature of the problem. Choosing the correct one is important because the evidence, deadlines and possible fees differ.

Request to Correct an Obvious Error

This procedure concerns clear technical or administrative mistakes: a boundary entered incorrectly, a lawful act omitted from the database, or an area mapped in a way that plainly conflicts with the official record. The request explains the error and includes the documents that demonstrate it.

Who Is It For?

It is suitable when the dispute does not require a new scientific judgment about vegetation or the historic character of the land. If the authority must evaluate aerial photographs, land use and forest characteristics, a formal objection is usually the appropriate route.

Formal Objection

An objection challenges the substance of the classification. It is submitted within the applicable period, normally carries a fee based on area, and should be supported by titles, a survey, historical imagery and a reasoned technical report. A competent committee examines the case.

Step by Step

Locate the exact polygon and code on the official platform. Confirm the property boundaries, collect ownership and administrative records, and compare historical evidence. A forester then assesses whether the case is an obvious error or a substantive dispute and prepares the corresponding file.

Essential Documents

Typical material includes ownership deeds, a recent topographic survey in the required coordinate system, Cadastre details, relevant permits or administrative decisions, aerial photographs and the applicant’s identification. Requirements vary, so current official instructions must always be checked before submission.
